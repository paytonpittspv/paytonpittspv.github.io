Film from the side with the phone still (tripod or a steady hand) and the athlete's whole body in frame. Slow motion (120 or 240 fps) gives the most precise number.

  1. Step to the first frame with both feet off the ground, then tap Mark takeoff.
  2. Step to the first frame where a foot touches down, then tap Mark landing.
  1. Set a hurdle right where the athlete jumps, the same distance from the camera, and enter its height.
  2. Tap the top of the hurdle bar, then the ground at its base.
  3. On a frame where the athlete stands tall, tap their hip (hands on hips makes this easy).
  4. Step to the highest point of the jump and tap the same spot on the hip.

Log a jump

Log more than one jump for the same athlete on the same day and the best one counts.

How often: test 1 to 3 times a week, not every practice. Two set days works for most teams, like Monday and Thursday. Monthly is too far apart to catch fatigue (that's what Testing Day is for), and every practice adds load and day-to-day noise, though daily checks can make sense in a heavy block or meet week. Flags start after two sessions and get more reliable once an athlete has five, about 2 to 3 weeks at twice a week. Test at the same point each time: after the same warm-up, before the hard work, 2 or 3 jumps with hands on hips, and log the best one.
How the flag works: baseline is the average of an athlete's previous five sessions (at least two needed). A drop past your threshold is flagged red, and a drop past half of it is yellow. It's a prompt to check in with the athlete, not a diagnosis.
